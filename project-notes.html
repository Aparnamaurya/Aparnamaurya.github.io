Project notes

What I built, what changed, and what I learned.

Short reflections on selected AI projects. Outcomes below are drawn from my CV; the learning notes summarize the engineering principles I took from this work.

Evaluation · Agentic AI

Making agentic AI quality measurable

The work

At ServiceNow, I helped design an enterprise-scale evaluation framework for customer-facing agentic AI. I contributed three of the four core metrics that are now deployed for customer-facing evaluation, and developed an LLM-as-a-Judge approach to standardize performance tracking.

Achievement

The framework brought a consistent evaluation approach into the product workflow and helped teams move from experiments toward production with clearer performance signals. I also built lifecycle components spanning data curation, fine-tuning, testing, and deployment.

What I learned

Evaluation is part of the product, not a final testing step. Useful metrics need to reflect real user outcomes, stay interpretable to cross-functional teams, and be repeatable as models and workflows change. Automated judging can help scale evaluation, but it needs deliberate calibration and human oversight.

Retrieval · Generative AI

Grounding an e-commerce assistant in useful product context

The work

I developed a Shopify e-commerce assistant combining generative AI with Elasticsearch-based retrieval. It supported product recommendations and customer interactions such as order tracking and personalized suggestions.

Achievement

The solution achieved a 40% improvement in recommendation precision and a 25% increase in customer retention.

What I learned

Retrieval quality is central to a useful generative experience: fluent output is not enough if the retrieved context is irrelevant. Product relevance and customer outcomes also need to be evaluated together, so improvements in model behavior translate into a better shopping experience.

Search · Knowledge systems

Making organizational knowledge easier to find

The work

I helped build an employee onboarding and knowledge platform that brought PDF, text, audio, and video resources into a searchable experience, using Elasticsearch for information retrieval.

Achievement

The platform reduced time-to-productivity for new hires by 50% by helping them find relevant resources through self-service search.

What I learned

Search across different media formats is as much a data and indexing problem as a user-interface problem. A useful knowledge tool depends on making content consistently discoverable and connecting search relevance to the task people are trying to complete.

NLP · Customer insight

Turning long-form customer feedback into timely insight

The work

I developed a GCP-based NLP pipeline for airline customer feedback. It used named entity recognition and aspect-based sentiment analysis to identify subjects and sentiment in long-form responses.

Achievement

The analysis turnaround was reduced from weeks to three hours, helping teams access customer insight much sooner.

What I learned

Useful sentiment analysis needs to explain what customers are reacting to, not just label a whole message as positive or negative. Pairing aspect identification with sentiment makes results easier to connect to service decisions, while deployment choices matter for keeping analysis timely and practical.

Computer vision · Privacy

Automating sensitive-content redaction in presentations

The work

I engineered a system to detect and redact sensitive PowerPoint content. The approach combined NER for text with object detection for images and was deployed as serverless inference on AWS.

Achievement

The system reduced manual effort by 70% while supporting privacy and security requirements.

What I learned

Documents are multimodal: important information may appear in text, images, or charts, so a single detection method is unlikely to cover every case. For privacy-sensitive automation, reliable review paths and careful evaluation are as important as detection performance.

Machine learning · Time series

Exploring financial prediction with price and news signals

The work

For my B.Tech project, I compared Random Forests, deep neural networks, LSTMs, and Bi-LSTMs for stock prediction using large-scale price and news data. I explored sentiment signals alongside time-series information.

Achievement

The project gave me hands-on experience comparing classical machine-learning models with recurrent deep-learning approaches and combining textual and temporal features.

What I learned

Model comparisons are only meaningful when the data and evaluation setup are consistent. Financial time series are noisy, and adding more signals does not automatically improve predictions; careful baselines and honest evaluation are essential to interpreting results.
